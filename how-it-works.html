How LifeCost Works: Understanding the Real Cost of a Purchase

LifeCost is a browser-based purchase cost calculator that helps you estimate how much an item may cost over the period you use it. Instead of looking only at the initial purchase price, it considers expected lifespan, usage, maintenance and resale value.

You enter your own estimates, and LifeCost uses them to calculate different cost metrics. These results can help you understand the expenses associated with a purchase and compare two options.

1. Enter the purchase price

The purchase price is the amount you pay or expect to pay for an item. It is the starting point for calculating its estimated ownership cost.

For example, if you purchase a laptop for ₹60,000, enter 60000 in the purchase price field.

Use the actual price you expect to pay rather than an unrelated advertised price or an old estimate.

2. Enter the expected lifespan

Expected lifespan is the number of years you plan to use the item before replacing it or finishing its useful life.

For example, if you expect to use a laptop for four years, enter 4 in the expected life field.

Think about product durability, your usage habits and whether the item will continue meeting your needs. The lifespan is an estimate, so the actual period may be different.

3. Estimate weekly usage

Weekly usage represents approximately how many times you expect to use the product each week.

For example:

  • A laptop used for study or work on 20 occasions each week: 20 uses.
  • Shoes worn three times each week: 3 uses.
  • A gym membership used four times each week: 4 visits.

Try to use realistic estimates. If your usage changes significantly over time, the calculated total uses may differ from your actual usage.

4. Add annual maintenance expenses

Maintenance is the estimated amount you spend each year to keep a product usable.

Examples include:

  • Laptop servicing and battery replacement.
  • Vehicle servicing and replacement parts.
  • Appliance repairs and filter replacements.
  • Cleaning and minor repairs for everyday products.

Enter the estimated maintenance cost per year. LifeCost multiplies this amount by the expected lifespan to calculate total maintenance.

Total maintenance = Annual maintenance × Expected lifespan

If you do not expect maintenance expenses, you can enter zero.

5. Enter the expected resale value

Resale value is the estimated amount you may receive if you sell the product after using it.

For example, if you purchase a laptop for ₹60,000 and expect to sell it for ₹10,000 after four years, enter 10000 as the expected resale value.

Resale value is subtracted from the purchase price and total maintenance when calculating net cost.

If you do not expect to resell the product, enter zero. Actual resale value depends on the item's condition, age, demand and market conditions.

6. Understand how LifeCost calculates net cost

After collecting the inputs, LifeCost calculates the estimated net cost over the expected ownership period.

Estimated net cost = Purchase price + Total maintenance − Expected resale value

For example, consider these laptop details:

  • Purchase price: ₹60,000
  • Expected lifespan: 4 years
  • Annual maintenance: ₹3,000
  • Expected resale value: ₹10,000

Total maintenance = ₹3,000 × 4 = ₹12,000.

Net cost = ₹60,000 + ₹12,000 − ₹10,000.

Estimated net cost = ₹62,000.

7. Understand the results displayed by LifeCost

After you select Calculate real cost, LifeCost displays several metrics based on your inputs.

Net cost

This is the estimated ownership cost after adding total maintenance and subtracting expected resale value.

Cost per use

This estimates how much each use of the product costs.

Cost per use = Net cost ÷ Estimated total uses

Cost per day

This divides the estimated net cost by the total number of days in the expected ownership period.

Cost per day = Net cost ÷ (Years × 365)

Cost per month

This shows the average estimated ownership cost per month.

Cost per month = Net cost ÷ (Years × 12)

Average cost per year

This shows the average estimated net cost for each year of ownership.

Average cost per year = Net cost ÷ Years

Estimated total uses

This represents the total number of uses expected throughout the ownership period.

Total uses = Uses per week × 52 × Years

8. Complete worked example: Laptop

Let's put all the inputs and formulas together using one hypothetical laptop purchase.

Purchase information

  • Purchase price: ₹60,000
  • Expected lifespan: 4 years
  • Weekly usage: 20 times
  • Annual maintenance: ₹3,000
  • Expected resale value: ₹10,000

Calculation 1: Total maintenance

₹3,000 × 4 = ₹12,000.

Calculation 2: Net cost

₹60,000 + ₹12,000 − ₹10,000 = ₹62,000.

Calculation 3: Total estimated uses

20 × 52 × 4 = 4,160 uses.

Calculation 4: Cost per use

₹62,000 ÷ 4,160 = approximately ₹14.90.

Calculation 5: Average cost per day

₹62,000 ÷ (4 × 365) = approximately ₹42.47.

Calculation 6: Average cost per month

₹62,000 ÷ 48 = approximately ₹1,291.67.

Calculation 7: Average cost per year

₹62,000 ÷ 4 = ₹15,500.

These figures show different ways of understanding the same estimated ownership cost. They are based entirely on the example inputs.

9. How the comparison tool works

LifeCost also includes a comparison feature for evaluating two purchases side by side.

Enter the following information for both purchases:

  • Product name.
  • Purchase price.
  • Expected lifespan.
  • Uses per week.
  • Annual maintenance expenses.
  • Expected resale value.

Select Compare purchases to see the calculated purchase price, net cost, cost per use, daily cost, monthly cost, yearly cost and estimated total uses for both options.

Using comparable and realistic assumptions makes the comparison more meaningful. The tool displays the calculated differences but does not decide which product you should purchase.

11. Important limitations to understand

LifeCost provides estimates using the information entered by the visitor. It does not automatically know the actual future expenses or usage of a product.

The main calculator does not include every possible ownership expense. For example, the following factors may need to be considered separately:

  • Taxes and delivery charges.
  • Financing costs and interest.
  • Unexpected repairs.
  • Insurance and replacement expenses.
  • Changes in product usage.
  • Changes in resale value.

The result should be treated as a planning estimate, not a guaranteed future cost or financial recommendation.

Frequently asked questions

Does LifeCost store my calculator inputs?

The main purchase calculator performs its calculations in your browser. The inputs are processed by the page's JavaScript to display the results.

What happens if I do not enter maintenance or resale value?

Empty maintenance and resale fields are treated as zero by the calculator. You can enter estimates when those expenses apply to your purchase.

Why is weekly usage required?

Weekly usage is needed to estimate the total number of uses and calculate cost per use. Enter a realistic number based on your expected usage.

Can I calculate costs for different types of products?

Yes. You can use the main calculator for products such as laptops, phones, shoes, appliances and other purchases, provided you can estimate their lifespan and usage.

Does LifeCost automatically recommend the cheapest product?

No. LifeCost displays estimated cost metrics based on your inputs. Product quality, reliability, features and personal requirements should also be considered.

Are the results exact?

No. The calculations depend on estimates of lifespan, usage, maintenance and resale value. Actual expenses and usage can differ.
