How to Compare Two Products Before Buying

Choosing between two products can be difficult when they have different prices, features and expected lifespans. A lower purchase price does not always mean a lower cost over time.

A structured comparison can help you understand what you may spend during ownership, how frequently you expect to use each product and which features matter for your needs.

1. Start with the purchase price

The purchase price is the initial amount you pay to own a product. It is an important starting point, but it should not be the only factor in your comparison.

For example, imagine two laptops:

  • Laptop A costs ₹60,000.
  • Laptop B costs ₹45,000.

Laptop B is ₹15,000 cheaper initially. However, the difference in price alone does not tell you which laptop offers a lower estimated ownership cost.

2. Compare the expected lifespan

Expected lifespan is the period you believe you can use a product before replacing it. Products with different lifespans should be compared carefully.

For example, a laptop expected to last four years may provide more time for use than one expected to last three years.

When estimating lifespan, consider:

  • Product build quality and durability.
  • How frequently you plan to use it.
  • Availability of repairs and replacement parts.
  • Whether its performance will meet your future needs.

Use realistic estimates rather than assuming that every product will last for its maximum possible lifespan.

3. Include maintenance and repair costs

Some products require regular servicing, repairs or replacement parts. These expenses can increase the total amount you spend during ownership.

Examples include:

  • Laptops: Battery replacement, repairs and servicing.
  • Vehicles: Fuel, servicing, tyres and replacement parts.
  • Home appliances: Filters, repairs and routine maintenance.
  • Shoes: Cleaning, polishing and minor repairs.

Estimate maintenance expenses over the entire ownership period, not just for one year.

Total maintenance = Annual maintenance × Years of use

4. Consider the expected resale value

Some products can be sold after you finish using them. The estimated amount you receive from selling a product can reduce its net ownership cost.

For example, if you purchase a laptop for ₹60,000 and later sell it for an estimated ₹10,000, the resale amount can be subtracted when calculating net cost.

Resale value depends on the product's age, condition, demand and market prices. It is an estimate, not guaranteed income.

5. Calculate the estimated net cost

Once you have estimated the purchase price, maintenance and resale value, calculate the net cost of each product.

Estimated net cost = Purchase price + Total maintenance − Resale value

Use the same calculation for both products. This makes it easier to understand their estimated ownership costs.

6. Compare the cost per use

Cost per use estimates how much an item costs each time you use it. It is particularly helpful when two products have different prices or expected lifespans.

First estimate the total number of uses:

Total uses = Uses per week × 52 × Years of use

Then calculate:

Cost per use = Estimated net cost ÷ Total estimated uses

For a fair comparison, use realistic weekly usage estimates for each product. The same usage frequency is appropriate when you expect to use both products equally often.

7. Worked example: Comparing two laptops

Consider two hypothetical laptops. The following example uses estimated prices, maintenance expenses and resale values to demonstrate how their costs can be compared.

Laptop A

  • Purchase price: ₹60,000
  • Expected lifespan: 4 years
  • Uses per week: 20
  • Annual maintenance: ₹3,000
  • Expected resale value: ₹10,000

Total maintenance = ₹3,000 × 4 = ₹12,000.

Net cost = ₹60,000 + ₹12,000 − ₹10,000 = ₹62,000.

Total uses = 20 × 52 × 4 = 4,160.

Cost per use = ₹62,000 ÷ 4,160 = approximately ₹14.90.

Laptop B

  • Purchase price: ₹45,000
  • Expected lifespan: 3 years
  • Uses per week: 20
  • Annual maintenance: ₹2,500
  • Expected resale value: ₹7,000

Total maintenance = ₹2,500 × 3 = ₹7,500.

Net cost = ₹45,000 + ₹7,500 − ₹7,000 = ₹45,500.

Total uses = 20 × 52 × 3 = 3,120.

Cost per use = ₹45,500 ÷ 3,120 = approximately ₹14.58.

8. Side-by-side comparison

Here is a summary of the two hypothetical laptops using the same calculation method.

Purchase price ₹60,000 ₹45,000

Resale value ₹10,000 ₹7,000

In this example, Laptop B has a slightly lower estimated cost per use, while Laptop A has a lower average cost per use difference of only about ₹0.32 in comparison with Laptop B.

Laptop B also has a lower estimated total net cost, but Laptop A has a longer expected lifespan. Neither calculation tells you which product has better performance or features.

9. Look beyond the calculated numbers

A financial comparison is only one part of choosing between products. Before making a purchase, consider other factors that may affect your experience.

  • Quality: Does the product meet your expected standards?
  • Performance: Does it have the features and performance you need?
  • Warranty: What coverage is included and for how long?
  • Reliability: Is it suitable for regular use?
  • Compatibility: Will it work with your existing devices or requirements?
  • Budget: Can you comfortably afford the initial purchase?

A product with a slightly higher cost per use may still be more suitable if it better meets your requirements.

10. Common mistakes when comparing products

  • Choosing a product only because its initial price is lower.
  • Using unrealistic lifespan estimates.
  • Ignoring maintenance and repair expenses.
  • Assuming the resale value will remain constant.
  • Comparing products using inconsistent usage assumptions.
  • Ignoring quality, warranty and product features.
  • Treating estimated calculations as guaranteed future expenses.

Frequently asked questions

Should I always choose the product with the lowest cost per use?

No. Cost per use does not measure product quality, performance, comfort or suitability. Consider these factors alongside the estimated cost.

Can two products have the same cost per use?

Yes. Products with different purchase prices and lifespans can have similar estimated costs per use.

Why should I compare the lifespan of products?

Lifespan affects the number of times you can use a product and the period over which you may need to pay for maintenance.

Should I include resale value in every comparison?

Include an estimated resale value when you reasonably expect to sell the product. Otherwise, you can enter zero.

Does LifeCost recommend which product to buy?

No. LifeCost calculates estimates based on your inputs and displays the results. You make the final decision based on your budget, needs and product requirements.

Are the comparison results guaranteed?

No. The results depend on estimated lifespan, usage, maintenance and resale value. Actual expenses and usage may differ.
