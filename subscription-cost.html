Why Subscriptions Add Up: Understanding Their Long-Term Cost

Subscriptions make it possible to access services by paying a recurring fee instead of making a large one-time purchase. Streaming platforms, gym memberships, cloud storage and software applications are common examples.

A monthly subscription may seem inexpensive on its own. However, when payments continue for several months or years, the total expense can become much larger than expected.

Understanding the total cost and how frequently you use a service can help you evaluate whether a subscription is worth keeping.

1. Understand the monthly subscription price

The monthly subscription price is the recurring amount charged for access to a service. Some services offer monthly plans, while others provide annual or longer-term payment options.

For example, imagine a subscription that costs ₹499 per month.

  • Monthly payment: ₹499
  • Three-month cost: ₹1,497
  • Six-month cost: ₹2,994
  • One-year cost: ₹5,988

Although ₹499 may appear to be a small monthly expense, paying it regularly results in a larger yearly total.

2. Calculate the cost over several months and years

A simple way to understand subscription spending is to multiply the monthly price by the number of months you expect to keep the service.

Total subscription cost = Monthly price × Number of months

Worked example: A ₹499 monthly subscription

Suppose you subscribe to a service that charges ₹499 every month and keep it for two years.

  • Monthly subscription price: ₹499
  • Number of months: 24
  • Total cost = ₹499 × 24

Total subscription cost = ₹11,976.

This calculation assumes the monthly price stays the same for all 24 months and there are no additional charges.

3. Estimate the cost per use

The total subscription cost tells you how much you pay over time. Cost per use gives you another way to understand the expense by considering how frequently you use the service.

Cost per use = Monthly subscription price ÷ Number of uses per month

Example: Streaming subscription

Imagine you pay ₹499 per month for a streaming service and watch something eight times each month.

  • Monthly subscription: ₹499
  • Estimated monthly uses: 8
  • Cost per use = ₹499 ÷ 8

Estimated cost per use = ₹62.38.

If you use the same service 20 times per month, the estimated cost per use becomes approximately ₹24.95.

This shows how usage frequency changes the effective cost of a subscription, even when the monthly payment remains unchanged.

4. Examples of recurring subscriptions

Streaming services

Video and music streaming services charge recurring fees for access to their content. If you subscribe to several services, their combined monthly payments can become a significant expense.

For example, three hypothetical subscriptions costing ₹199, ₹299 and ₹499 per month would have a combined monthly cost of ₹997.

Over 12 months, the combined cost would be:

₹997 × 12 = ₹11,964.

Gym memberships

A gym membership may be charged monthly, quarterly or annually. The number of visits you make affects its effective cost per visit.

For example, if a gym membership costs ₹12,000 for one year and you visit four times per week:

  • Annual membership cost: ₹12,000
  • Estimated annual visits: 4 × 52 = 208
  • Cost per visit: ₹12,000 ÷ 208

Estimated cost per visit = ₹57.69.

If you visit only twice per week, the estimated cost per visit increases to approximately ₹115.38.

Software subscriptions

Software subscriptions may provide access to productivity tools, design applications, cloud services or other features.

For example, a hypothetical software plan costing ₹799 per month would cost ₹9,588 over 12 months, assuming the price remains unchanged.

When evaluating software subscriptions, consider how frequently you use the application and whether its features are necessary for your work or studies.

5. Understand multiple subscriptions together

People sometimes subscribe to multiple services without considering their combined cost. Looking at each payment separately can make recurring expenses seem smaller than they are.

Consider these hypothetical monthly subscriptions:

  • Streaming service: ₹499
  • Music service: ₹199
  • Cloud storage: ₹130
  • Software subscription: ₹799

Total monthly subscription cost:

₹499 + ₹199 + ₹130 + ₹799 = ₹1,627.

Estimated yearly cost:

₹1,627 × 12 = ₹19,524.

This example illustrates how several small recurring payments can combine into a larger annual expense.

6. Review your recurring subscriptions

Reviewing subscriptions periodically can help you understand which services you actually use and which ones may no longer be necessary.

Here are some useful questions to ask:

  • Have I used this service during the last month?
  • Do I need all the features included in the current plan?
  • Am I paying for another service that offers similar features?
  • Would a different billing plan suit my usage?
  • Will I continue using this service over the next few months?

Check the actual billing frequency, renewal date and cancellation conditions before making changes to a subscription.

7. Monthly plans versus annual plans

Some subscription providers offer a discounted annual payment option alongside a monthly plan. Comparing the total payments can help you understand the difference.

For example, consider a hypothetical service with these plans:

  • Monthly plan: ₹499 per month.
  • Annual plan: ₹4,999 for one year.

Monthly payments over one year would cost ₹499 × 12 = ₹5,988.

The annual plan would cost ₹4,999.

Difference = ₹5,988 − ₹4,999 = ₹989.

The annual plan has a lower stated cost in this example. However, it requires a larger upfront payment and may be less suitable if you do not expect to use the service for the entire year.

8. Common subscription cost mistakes

  • Looking only at the monthly payment instead of the yearly total.
  • Forgetting about automatic renewals.
  • Paying for multiple services with overlapping features.
  • Assuming you will use a service more frequently than you actually do.
  • Ignoring price changes, taxes or additional charges.
  • Choosing an annual plan without considering whether you will continue using it.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate the yearly cost of a monthly subscription?

Multiply the monthly subscription price by 12. For example, a ₹499 monthly subscription costs ₹5,988 over 12 months if the price remains unchanged.

How do I calculate the cost per use of a subscription?

Divide the monthly subscription price by the estimated number of times you use the service each month.

Are annual subscription plans always cheaper?

Not necessarily. Compare the actual annual payment with the total monthly payments. Also consider upfront payment requirements, cancellation terms and how long you expect to use the service.

Should I cancel a subscription I rarely use?

Review whether the service provides enough value for your needs. Consider how often you use it, whether you need its features and whether an alternative plan is available.

Does LifeCost track my subscriptions automatically?

No. LifeCost's subscription tool uses the information you enter to calculate estimated costs. It does not connect to your bank account or automatically monitor recurring payments.

Are subscription cost estimates guaranteed?

No. Calculations are based on your inputs. Actual expenses can change because of price increases, taxes, promotions, billing changes or additional charges.
